SETUP FOR HUMANS
================

You need
--------
- A Raspberry Pi Zero W, a microSD card (16 GB or bigger), and a good
  power supply (5 volt, 2.5 amp). Weak chargers make the wifi unstable.
- A mini-HDMI to HDMI cable for the TV.
- A computer (Mac or Windows) for the one-time setup.
- A phone hotspot (or home wifi) with internet, for the one-time setup.
  IMPORTANT: the Pi Zero only sees 2.4 GHz wifi.
  iPhone: Settings > Personal Hotspot > turn ON "Maximize Compatibility".
  Android: Hotspot settings > Band > 2.4 GHz.

Step 1 - Put the system on the memory card (about 20-30 minutes)
----------------------------------------------------------------
1. On the computer, install "Raspberry Pi Imager" from raspberrypi.com.
2. Open it and choose:
     Device:  Raspberry Pi Zero
     OS:      Raspberry Pi OS (other) > Raspberry Pi OS Lite (32-bit)
     Storage: your memory card
3. When it asks about settings, choose "Edit settings" and fill in:
     Hostname:  pitv
     Username and password: anything you'll remember
     Wifi: your hotspot's name and password, typed exactly
     Wifi country: your country
     Services tab: turn ON SSH, use password
4. Write. It downloads, writes, then checks the card. The progress bar
   starting again from 0 is the checking step, that's normal.

Step 2 - Start the Pi
---------------------
1. Turn your hotspot on.
2. Put the card in the Pi and plug in power (the port marked PWR).
3. Wait about 3-5 minutes. The first start is slow, it sets itself up.

Step 3 - Install the program (about 15-20 minutes)
--------------------------------------------------
Put the computer on the same hotspot. Then:

Mac: open the Terminal app, drag the "send-to-pi.sh" file from this
folder into the Terminal window, type a space, then type your username
@pitv.local, for example:

    /Users/you/PiPhotoTV/send-to-pi.sh raspberry@pitv.local

and press Enter. Type the Pi's password when asked (nothing shows while
you type, that's normal). When the installer asks for the hotspot name,
just press Enter - the Pi already knows it from Step 1.

Windows: open PowerShell and run these, one at a time (replace raspberry
with your username):

    scp -r C:\path\to\PiPhotoTV raspberry@pitv.local:~
    ssh -t raspberry@pitv.local "cd PiPhotoTV && sudo ./install.sh"

At the end the Pi restarts by itself. Done!

Step 4 - Use it
---------------
1. Plug the Pi into the TV. Photos start playing (or a welcome screen
   that explains what to do, if there are no photos yet).
2. On a phone, open wifi settings and join "raspberry pi tv".
3. A page pops up. Tap "Choose photos" and pick photos. Keep the screen
   on until it says "All done".
   - On iPhone, after tapping "Add" the phone prepares all the photos
     before anything happens. With 50+ photos that can take a few
     minutes. Picking about 20 at a time is quickest - you can pick more
     while the first ones are sending.
   - If the popup page doesn't open your photos: tap "Stay connected",
     then open Safari or Chrome and go to  192.168.4.1
   - People on the hotspot instead can go to  http://pitv.local
4. To look at, save or delete photos on the phone: tap "Photos" at the
   bottom and type the password (default: laura).
5. To add another wifi for the Pi, or change how long each photo stays
   on screen: tap "Settings" (same password).

If something seems wrong
------------------------
- Nothing on the TV: make sure the TV is on the right HDMI input, then
  unplug the Pi's power and plug it back in.
- The wifi "raspberry pi tv" is missing: unplug the power, wait 10
  seconds, plug it back in, wait 2 minutes.
- "Some photos couldn't be opened and were skipped": those were usually
  only in iCloud. Open them in the Photos app first so they download,
  then send them again.
- Turning the Pi off: just unplug it. It's built to survive that.
