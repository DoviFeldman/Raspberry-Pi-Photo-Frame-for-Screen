SETUP FOR HUMANS
================

First, pick a version (see README.txt):
  HOME WIFI version  - the house where the TV is has wifi. (Most people.)
  HOME WIFI + TAILSCALE - same, and family far away can send photos too.
  OWN WIFI version   - no wifi there; the Pi makes its own "raspberry pi tv".

You need
--------
- A Raspberry Pi with wifi (Zero W, Zero 2 W, 3 A+/B/B+, or 4), a microSD
  card (16 GB or bigger), and a good power supply (5 volt, 2.5 amp or
  more). Weak chargers make the wifi unstable. A case and a surge
  protector help it last.
- An HDMI cable for the TV (the Zero needs a mini-HDMI to HDMI cable).
- A Mac (or Windows PC) for the one-time setup.
- A phone hotspot (or home wifi) with internet, for the one-time setup.
  IMPORTANT for a Pi Zero W: it only sees 2.4 GHz wifi.
  iPhone: Settings > Personal Hotspot > turn ON "Maximize Compatibility".
  Android: Hotspot settings > Band > 2.4 GHz.

Step 1 - Put the system on the memory card (about 20-30 minutes)
----------------------------------------------------------------
1. On the computer, install "Raspberry Pi Imager" from raspberrypi.com.
2. Open it and choose:
     Device:  your Pi model
     OS:      Raspberry Pi OS (other) > Raspberry Pi OS Lite (32-bit)
     Storage: your memory card
3. When it asks about settings, choose "Edit settings" and fill in:
     Hostname:  pitv
     Username and password: anything you'll remember
     Wifi: your hotspot's name and password, typed exactly
     Wifi country: your country
     Services tab: turn ON SSH, use password
4. Write. It downloads, writes, then checks the card. The progress bar
   starting again from 0 is the checking step, that's normal.

Step 2 - Start the Pi
---------------------
1. Turn your hotspot on.
2. Put the card in the Pi and plug in power (the port marked PWR).
3. Wait about 3-5 minutes. The first start is slow, it sets itself up.

Step 3 - Install the program (about 5-20 minutes)
-------------------------------------------------
Put the computer on the same hotspot. Then:

Mac: open the Terminal app, drag the "send-to-pi.sh" file from this
folder into the Terminal window, type a space, then type your username
@pitv.local. For the OWN WIFI version add  --own-wifi  at the end, for
the TAILSCALE version add  --tailscale  at the end.
For example:

    /Users/you/PiPhotoTV/send-to-pi.sh raspberry@pitv.local
    /Users/you/PiPhotoTV/send-to-pi.sh raspberry@pitv.local --own-wifi

and press Enter. Type the Pi's password when asked (nothing shows while
you type, that's normal). The installer then asks:
  - HOME WIFI version: the house wifi's name and password (the wifi at
    the house where the TV will be). You can skip it and add it later.
  - Both: which hotspot to join by itself. Just press Enter - the Pi
    already knows it from Step 1.

Windows: open PowerShell and run these, one at a time (replace raspberry
with your username; add --own-wifi after install.sh for that version):

    scp -r C:\path\to\PiPhotoTV raspberry@pitv.local:~
    ssh -t raspberry@pitv.local "cd PiPhotoTV && sudo ./install.sh"

At the end the Pi restarts by itself. Done!

Step 4 - Use it: HOME WIFI version
----------------------------------
1. Plug the Pi into the TV. Photos start playing (or a welcome screen
   that shows the website address, if there are no photos yet). For the
   first 3 minutes after it's turned on, the address also shows in the
   bottom corner of the TV, for example  pitv.local  or  192.168.1.37
2. On a phone on the same house wifi, open Safari or Chrome and go to
   pitv.local  (if that doesn't open, type the number address from the
   TV instead).
3. Tap "Choose photos" and pick photos. Keep the screen on until it says
   "All done".
4. Make it an icon on the phone, so next time it's one tap:
   - iPhone: in Safari tap the Share button, then "Add to Home Screen".
   - Android: in Chrome tap the 3 dots menu, then "Add to Home screen".
   Add it from the pitv.local address if that one works on the phone.

Step 4 - Extra for the TAILSCALE version
----------------------------------------
1. During the install a link appears. Open it and log in to your
   Tailscale account (free, make one at tailscale.com). At the end the
   installer prints the Pi's Tailscale address, like http://100.x.y.z
2. At login.tailscale.com/admin/machines, on the Pi: "..." > "Disable key
   expiry", or it stops working after 6 months.
3. For each person far away: on the Pi click "..." > "Share", send them
   the link. They install the Tailscale app, sign in, accept the share,
   and turn Tailscale on.
4. They open the Pi's Tailscale address in Safari or Chrome (with the
   Tailscale app on) and send photos. "Add to Home Screen" works here too.

Step 4 - Use it: OWN WIFI version
---------------------------------
1. Plug the Pi into the TV. Photos start playing (or a welcome screen
   that explains what to do, if there are no photos yet).
2. On a phone, open wifi settings and join "raspberry pi tv".
3. A page pops up. Tap "Choose photos" and pick photos. Keep the screen
   on until it says "All done".
   - If the popup page doesn't open your photos: tap "Stay connected",
     then open Safari or Chrome and go to  192.168.4.1
   - People on the hotspot instead can go to  http://pitv.local

Both versions
-------------
- On iPhone, after tapping "Add" the phone prepares all the photos
  before anything happens. With 50+ photos that can take a few minutes,
  and faster wifi doesn't change that part (it happens inside the phone).
  Picking about 20 at a time is quickest - you can pick more while the
  first ones are sending.
- To look at, save or delete photos on the phone: tap "Photos" at the
  bottom and type the password (default: laura).
- To add another wifi for the Pi, or change how long each photo stays
  on screen: tap "Settings" (same password).

If something seems wrong
------------------------
- Nothing on the TV: make sure the TV is on the right HDMI input, then
  unplug the Pi's power and plug it back in.
- HOME WIFI version, the page won't open: make sure the phone is on the
  house wifi (not on cell data or a hotspot), and try the number address
  shown on the TV. Unplugging the Pi and plugging it back in shows the
  address on the TV again for 3 minutes. If the house wifi's password
  changed, turn on your phone hotspot - the Pi joins it, and you can add
  the new password on the Settings page from http://pitv.local
- OWN WIFI version, the wifi "raspberry pi tv" is missing: unplug the
  power, wait 10 seconds, plug it back in, wait 2 minutes.
- "Some photos couldn't be opened and were skipped": those were usually
  only in iCloud. Open them in the Photos app first so they download,
  then send them again.
- Turning the Pi off: just unplug it. It's built to survive that.
